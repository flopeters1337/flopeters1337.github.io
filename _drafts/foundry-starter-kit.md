If you're an avid fan of roleplaying games and especially if you have been forced to switch your gaming sessions online due to last 
year's pandemic, you probably have heard of *virtual tabletop* software.
There are a bunch of options out there to bring your friends and TTRPG enthusiasts together online: Roll20, Fantasy Grounds, Astral, and even Tabletop Simulator are all potential candidates for your weekly Dungeon & Dragoning.
One piece of software in particular has seen a surge in popularity: *Foundry VTT*.
There are multiple advantages for using Foundry over other virtual tabletops but the main ones are:
- One time purchase instead of a subscription business model.
- High flexibility due to its community modules support.
- Free for players.
- Fast and snappy to use.
- Open source.

Foundry has established itself as the new norm for virtual tabletop software and we couldn't be happier about it!

However if there is one aspect of Foundry that is a bit lacking in my opinion at the moment is its documentation.
Don't get me wrong: the API documentation is exhaustive and for the most part helps you get on the right track.
However, as someone who has done little Javascript development in the past, I find the quick start guide to be too shallow and fails at introducing to the fundamental ideas behind Foundry's inner workings.
This article will be the first in a series of tutorials to help new Foundry developers get started with module development.

So strap up and let's slay some code dragons!
